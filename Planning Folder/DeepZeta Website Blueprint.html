The big idea

The website is our first case study

Every agency says "we build fast, AI-ready websites". Most of their own sites are slow templates. Ours has to show the proof live, on the page, in the visitor's own browser.

Who it is for

Owners and managers of UAE and GCC businesses (clinics, real estate, e-commerce, schools, service companies) who lose time on repetitive work and lose leads to slow follow-up.

What it must do

Turn visitors into booked free AI automation audits. Everything else (WhatsApp, calls, downloads, newsletter) is a secondary action.

How it wins

Visitors try the automations on the page: chat with our AI agent, calculate their savings, watch their own workflow get automated. Showing beats telling.

Showcase

Live demos visitors can try

This is how the site shows your automations instead of describing them. As your performance rules require, each demo lists its speed cost and how we keep that cost low.

1 · deepzeta AI agent

A chat assistant trained on our services, prices and case studies. It answers in Arabic or English, qualifies the visitor and books the audit into the calendar. The same product we sell, working live.

Cost
A chat widget can add 60–150 KB of script.
Fix
Show a lightweight button first and load the chat only when someone taps it. It adds nothing to page load.

2 · Automation ROI calculator

Visitors enter staff hours, lead volume and reply time, and see hours and dirhams saved per month. The result ends in "Book an audit to get this plan".

Cost
About 5 KB of script.
Fix
Plain JavaScript, loaded only on pages that use it. No libraries.

3 · Workflow explorer

Pick an industry (clinic, real estate, shop) and watch an animated diagram: lead arrives → AI replies → CRM updated → appointment booked, with time saved at each step.

Cost
Animation can slow low-end phones.
Fix
Animated SVG and CSS only. It pauses when off screen and shows a static diagram when reduced motion is on.

4 · 60-second speed-to-lead test

The visitor leaves a number and gets a WhatsApp reply from our automation within a minute. This is the strongest proof we can offer, because they feel it on their own phone.

Cost
Nothing on the page. The work runs on the server (n8n plus the WhatsApp API).
Fix
Needs consent wording, rate limits and spam protection on the form.

5 · Live speed badge

A small footer badge showing how fast this page loaded in the visitor's browser, for example "This page loaded in 0.9 s". It proves the "fast website" claim with real data.

Cost
About 1 KB.
Fix
Uses the browser's built-in performance data after the page has loaded.

6 · "See how AI reads this page"

A toggle that shows the structured data and llms.txt that ChatGPT and Google read from the page. It proves the AEO and AI-citation service on our own site.

Cost
Close to zero. The data is already in the page.
Fix
Hidden until the visitor opens it.
Design discussion

Three design directions

All three use your logo, colours and the 70/20/10 balance (navy, white text, brand colour). They differ in mood and in how much motion they use. Pick one, or mix them.

Your business, running itself.
Recommended

A · Signal Grid

Navy with the faint blue grid from your colour system. The hero is the logo's Z ribbon, drawn in SVG, with a slow light sweep across it. Confident, premium and clearly "AI company".

Motion: one hero moment only. The ribbon is SVG, so it is also the fast-loading main image. An optional small WebGL shader loads later, only on strong devices.

B · Control Room

A dashboard look: live number tiles, workflow lines and a monospace data font. It feels like watching the automations run. Best for showing results and the demos.

Motion: small counters and flowing lines, all CSS. Risk: it can feel cold. Mix it with A for the demo and case-study sections.

Grow with AI.

C · Daylight

A light page with navy sections for contrast. Friendlier and more "business consultancy". It suits conservative sectors such as law and finance.

Motion: the least of the three. Risk: weaker link to your dark logo, and less "tech" feel.

My recommendation

Signal Grid (A) for the brand, with Control Room (B) for demos and results. Dark is the default look, as your rules ask, with a full light mode built from the same tokens so visitors can switch. The four pixel colours become the colour code for the four pillars across menus, icons and page headers.

Rules from your performance file

  • Only one 3D or WebGL moment on the whole site, and the page must work fully without it
  • Content reveals on scroll using CSS only, and the site never takes over the user's scrolling
  • Glass blur only on the menu bar, pop-ups and a few feature cards
  • Hover and button feedback everywhere, and no constant background animation
